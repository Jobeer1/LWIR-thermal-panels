Geometry: —

Wave optics & directional asymmetry: each Monte-Carlo photon is wavelength-sampled from the Planck distribution at TB / TA and tested against the channel's modal cutoff λc (TE11/TE10). When a pore's thermal wavelengths exceed its cutoff, deep thermal modes become evanescent: internally-emitted photons above λc become evanescent waves whose power decays as exp(−2L/δev) and fail to reach the aperture (LDOS suppression — Lin PRB 2000; Narayanaswamy & Chen PRB 2004). The reported propagating fraction and rim contribution are calculated for the selected geometry.

Incident light can be trapped at the thin wall rims while internally emitted photons are filtered by the deep cavity and modal cutoff. The model therefore reports separate effective quantities, αeff and εB, and their ratio as directional decoupling. Net heat flow is obtained from the radiosity balance, including the cold surroundings.
