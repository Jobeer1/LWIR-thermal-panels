Geometry: —

Wave optics & directional asymmetry: each Monte-Carlo photon is wavelength-sampled from the Planck distribution at TB / TA and tested against the channel's modal cutoff λc (TE11/TE10). Sub-wavelength pores (5 µm < λc = 8.53 µm at 300 K) make deep thermal modes evanescent: internally-emitted photons below λc become evanescent waves that decay as exp(−L/δev) and fail to reach the aperture (LDOS suppression — Lin PRB 2000; Narayanaswamy & Chen PRB 2004). Only the shallow rim strip (within one δev) and the 17.4% propagating spectral fraction escape, driving εB → 0.

Incident light, by contrast, diffracts around the thin — µm wall rims and is trapped by the graded-index surface (αeff → 1). This creates the operational decoupling: αeff ≫ εB — the hallmark of sub-wavelength structured emitters. Even at T_A = T_B, the emissivity mismatch (ε_A ≠ ε_B) means one-way radiation flows both ways (A→B: ~ε_A·F·α_eff·σT⁴, B→A: ~ε_B·F·σT⁴); the physical net A→B flux balances against the surroundings.
